## Introduction

Knowledge involves three fundamental elements:

- **Subject** — the one who knows.
- **Object** — that which is known.
- **Act of Knowing** — the cognitive relation between subject and object.

## Metaphysical Structure of Knowing

> **Metaphysically, the subject is, the object is and the cognition is.**

---

### Meaning

**The subject is a being is in insofar is it is or in the capacity of subject (Being qua Being in Aristotelian metaphysics term), the object is a being insofar as it is, and cognition is a being insofar as it is.**

---

### ⚠ Note

> subject, object, cognition are being insofar they in Being (Being of beings).

---

### Structural Emphasis

- **Subject** — being insofar as it is (in the capacity of subject)  
- **Object** — being insofar as it is  
- **Cognition** — being insofar as it is  

---


No knowledge seems to be possible without Being. Knowledge takes place in Being, through Being, and by Being. Here Being appears as the core and ground of knowledge itself, and hence of all knowledge, whether empirical or philosophical. Hence, metaphysics is implicit in all knowledge.



### Etymology of Metaphysics 

### Western
The term ‘metaphysics’ was coined by Andronicus of Rhodes (C. 70 B.C) for those collected works of Aristotle placed ‘after physics’ (*ta + meta + ta + phusika*).

Hence in the course of time ‘metaphysics’ (*ta+ meta + ta + phusika*) came to mean that department of philosophy which deals with those features of beings that are hyperphysical, super sensible, and immaterial.


### Indian 
The oldest term used in the Vedic literature to communicate it is *Brahmodya* (c. 9th century B.C). Brahmodya means speculative discussion about Brahman.


In the Upanishads (800-600 B.C) it came to be denoted by the words *atmavidya* (science or knowledge of the self), *paravidya* (supreme science or transcendental knowledge), and *brahmavidya* (science or knowledge of Brahman). In the oldest books of the Buddhist Pali canon, the word *ditthi (drshti)* stands for the metaphysical viewpoint. In the Gita, metaphysics is *adyatmavidya* (science of the self).



## General definition of metaphysics 


Metaphysics is the study of being as being. 




### 1. It underlies all other departments

Metaphysics underlies all other departments since its principles are the detached and disinterested drive of the pure desire to know. The unfolding of the pure desire to know takes place in the empirical, intellectual, and rational consciousness of the self-affirming subject.  

All questions, all insights, all formulations, all reflections, and all judgments proceed from the unfolding of that drive. Hence, metaphysics underlies logic, mathematics, and all other sciences.

---

### 2. It penetrates all other departments

Other departments are constituted of the same principles as that of metaphysics. They are particular departments related to particular viewpoints. Yet, all departments spring from a common source and seek a common compatibility and coherence.  

Hence, they are penetrated by metaphysics.

---

### 3. It transforms all other departments

Metaphysics originates from the experience of something. It is free from the realization of particular viewpoints. It distinguishes positions from counter-positions in the whole of knowledge.  

It is a transforming principle that urges positions to fuller development. By reversing counter-positions, it liberates discoveries from the shackles in which they were formulated.


### 4. It unifies all other departments

Other disciplines respond to particular ranges of questions, whereas metaphysics confronts the original and total question. It seeks not partial answers but the total answer.  

Metaphysics gathers and orders all other forms of knowledge into a coherent whole. It is the whole *in* knowledge, though not the whole *of* knowledge. Its principles are prior to all other sciences and integrate them into unity.

---

### 5. Metaphysics as the Core and Ground of Philosophy

Descartes compared philosophy to a tree:  
- **Roots** — Metaphysics  
- **Trunk** — Physics  
- **Branches** — Other sciences  

Heidegger suggested that the roots require a deeper ground — Being itself. Yet metaphysics cannot be separated from its ground, for its formal object is Being.  

Thus metaphysics is not merely the root of philosophy but also its ground, since Being itself constitutes and distinguishes it from all other sciences.

---

### 6. Metaphysics as Science

Human inquiry seeks more than superficial knowledge; it strives to grasp ultimate causes. Science, in its traditional sense, is certain knowledge through causes.  

Metaphysics qualifies as the highest science because it investigates:  
- The first efficient cause  
- The ultimate final cause  
- The necessary features of being  

Its starting point — *that there is something* — is immediately known. From this, metaphysics proceeds demonstratively through rational reflection.  

Unlike experimental sciences, metaphysics cannot verify its conclusions through sensible experimentation. Its verification lies in the rigor and coherence of its reasoning.  

Therefore, metaphysical conclusions are not hypotheses but demonstrated insights grounded in evident principles and necessary reasoning.

## Scope of metaphysics 

Metaphysics is study of material and formal objects. Usually

- Matter(Potentiality) : It indicate indeterminate but determinable element, the raw material capable of becoming something.

- Form(Actuality or essence) : It indicate determining element or essence of an element. 


The material object of metaphysics includes all things which fall under the notion of being, such as actual or possible, abstract or concrete, material or immaterial, finite or infinite. The formal object of metaphysics is the study of ‘being as being’.That is to say, metaphysics does not restrict itself to any particular being or part of that being, but rather treats what is common to all beings, namely Being which is the ground of beings since all beings are in Being.

### Being by means of judgement (Problem of one and many)

*Being pertains to everything*:  Being pertains to everything the real, necessary, possible, knowing subject or known object, even something that is in imagination. Hence it is a general notion *i.e.* it is one that refers to many.

*Being is absolutely general notion* because it is predicated absolutely to all. 

## The Notion of Being

### 1. Being Cannot Be Defined

A definition places a concept under a higher genus and distinguishes it by a specific difference.  

Being cannot be defined in this manner because there is no concept more universal than Being. It does not fall under a higher class and therefore is not a species.  

Being is the most general and fundamental notion.

Example : Human = Animal (genus) + rational (difference).

---

### 2. Being as the First Known

Being is presupposed in all acts of knowing.  

Whenever something is known, thought, or proposed, it is already understood as *being*. Thus Being is the first known — implicitly present in every act of cognition.

---

### 3. The Structure of Being

Though universal, the notion of Being shows a structural distinction:

- That which is (the bearer or subject)
- That by which it is (the act of being)

Being can function:

- As a **predicate**, emphasizing participation in existence.
- As a **noun**, emphasizing the entity that participates in existence.

---

### 4. Being and Abstraction

The question arises whether Being is known by abstraction. 

*Abstraction* is a something by which the mind leaves aside abstracts from certain aspect of a being.

*Total abstraction* : It means that the being from which the concept is abstracted is not expressed as to one of its parts only but as a whole: **the totality is expressed but not totally.**
