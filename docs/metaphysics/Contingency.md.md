# The Contingency Argument

## 1. The Basic Intuition

Some things exist, but they did not have to exist.

They depend on causes, conditions, or explanations outside themselves.
Such things are called **contingent beings**.

Examples include:
- Physical objects
- Biological organisms
- Events
- Possibly even the universe itself

---

## 2. The Principle of Sufficient Reason (PSR)

For every contingent fact, there is a reason why it is so rather than otherwise.

This is not an empirical claim but a metaphysical demand for intelligibility.

---

## 3. The Core Argument

1. Contingent beings exist.
2. Every contingent being requires an explanation.
3. The totality of contingent beings cannot explain itself.
4. Therefore, there must exist a **necessary being** whose reason for existence lies in itself.

---

## 4. Philosophical Status

This argument does not yet identify:
- God of religion
- A personal being
- Moral attributes

It establishes only the necessity of a **non-contingent ground of reality**.
